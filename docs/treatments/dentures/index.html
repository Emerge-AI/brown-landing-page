What Is a Denture — And Is One Right for You?

A denture — or complete denture as it is often called — is a removable appliance that is inserted in the mouth, replaces natural teeth, and provides vital support for the cheeks and lips. Modern dentures look more natural and feel more comfortable than ever before.

Most dentures are made of acrylic and can be fabricated two different ways: as a conventional denture, which is made after all teeth have been extracted and the gums have healed; or as an immediate denture, which is fabricated in advance and inserted the same day teeth are removed — so you're never without teeth during the healing period.

Dentures can also be fabricated to fit over endodontically treated teeth, and a complete denture can be attached to dental implants for a dramatically more secure fit. Dr. Brown will assess your specific situation and recommend the most appropriate type for your needs.

  • Replaces all or some missing natural teeth
  • Supports cheeks and lips to maintain facial structure
  • Restores your ability to eat, speak and smile with confidence
  • Custom-crafted for your unique mouth shape and bite
  • Implant-supported options available for superior stability
  • Regular checkups ensure fit is maintained as bone changes over time

Four Denture Options at Our Fort Worth Practice

Dr. Brown offers four distinct denture options to suit different clinical situations, timelines, and patient goals. During your consultation, he will recommend the most appropriate solution for your specific needs.

Conventional Full Denture (Most Common): A conventional denture is made after all remaining teeth have been extracted and the gum tissue has fully healed — typically 8 to 12 weeks. This healing period allows the gums to settle into their final shape, resulting in the most accurate, comfortable-fitting denture possible. Conventional dentures offer the best long-term fit and are the most widely prescribed option.

Immediate Denture (Never Without Teeth): An immediate denture is fabricated in advance and inserted on the same day the teeth are extracted — so the patient is never without teeth during the healing period. The tissues heal under the denture. Because the mouth continues to change shape during healing, immediate dentures typically require relining or replacement once healing is complete, but they are an excellent solution for patients who cannot be without teeth for social or professional reasons.

Partial Denture (Some Natural Teeth Remain): A partial denture is the solution when some natural teeth remain. It fills in the spaces left by missing teeth and is designed to attach to the existing healthy teeth using metal clasps or precision attachments. Partial dentures prevent remaining teeth from shifting into the gap and restore the appearance and function of a full smile — without requiring the removal of healthy teeth.

Implant-Supported Denture (Most Secure Option): A complete denture can be attached to dental implants placed in the jawbone to allow for a dramatically more secure, stable fit. Unlike conventional dentures that rely on suction and adhesives, implant-supported dentures snap firmly into place — eliminating slipping and discomfort during eating and speaking. They also help preserve the underlying jawbone and feel much closer to natural teeth.

  • Conventional — best long-term fit, made after gums have fully healed
  • Immediate — placed same day as extractions, no gap period without teeth
  • Partial — preserves remaining natural teeth and prevents shifting
  • Implant-supported — eliminates slipping and denture adhesives

Implant-Supported Dentures — A More Secure, Confident Fit

A complete denture can be attached to dental implants to allow for a dramatically more secure and comfortable fit. Unlike conventional dentures that can slip during eating or speaking, implant-supported dentures snap firmly into place on titanium posts anchored in the jawbone — staying put no matter what you eat or say.

Implant-supported dentures also stimulate the jawbone, preventing the bone loss that typically occurs after tooth extraction — preserving your facial structure and keeping your denture fitting correctly for years longer than a conventional denture alone.

  • Eliminate slipping and denture adhesives forever
  • Eat your favourite foods with confidence
  • Preserves jawbone and maintains facial structure
  • Feels dramatically more like natural teeth
  • Long-term solution — implants can last a lifetime

What to Expect as Your Dentures Age

Dentures over a normal course of time will wear and need to be replaced or relined in order to keep the jaw alignment correct. This is completely normal and expected — and understanding it helps you plan for long-term dental health.

The alignment changes slowly over time as the bone and gum ridges recede or shrink due to the extraction of the teeth. As the underlying structures change, the denture that once fit perfectly may begin to feel loose, uncomfortable, or cause sore spots. Relining reshapes the inner surface of the denture to match the new contours of your gums — restoring a comfortable, secure fit without fabricating an entirely new denture.

Regular dental examinations are still important for denture wearers — even when you have no natural teeth remaining. Dr. Brown checks the oral tissues for signs of disease or change, evaluates the fit and condition of your denture, and assesses whether relining or replacement is needed. Catching problems early prevents discomfort and more costly treatment later.

  • Relining — reshapes the inner surface to restore fit as gums change, less costly than a full replacement
  • Replacement — typically recommended every 5–10 years as wear and bone changes progress
  • Regular checkups — essential even without natural teeth; Dr. Brown checks tissues for disease and monitors fit
  • Facial support — a well-fitting denture maintains proper jaw alignment and prevents the 'sunken face' appearance from bone loss

Your Questions Answered

Frequently Asked Questions

What types of dentures does Dr. Brown offer?

Dr. Marshall H. Brown offers four types of dentures at our Fort Worth practice: conventional full dentures — made after all teeth are extracted and gums have healed (8–12 weeks); immediate dentures — placed the same day as extractions so you're never without teeth; partial dentures — for patients who still have some natural teeth remaining; and implant-supported dentures — attached to dental implants for a secure, stable fit without slipping. Dr. Brown will recommend the most appropriate type during your consultation based on your specific oral health situation.

What is the difference between a conventional and immediate denture?

A conventional denture is fabricated after all teeth have been extracted and the gum tissue has fully healed — typically 8 to 12 weeks. This results in the most accurate, comfortable-fitting denture possible. An immediate denture is made in advance and inserted on the same day the teeth are extracted, so the patient is never without teeth. The tissues heal under the denture. Because the mouth changes shape during healing, immediate dentures usually require relining or replacement once healing is complete.

How long do dentures last?

Dentures typically last 5 to 10 years before needing replacement. Over time, the bone and gum ridges change shape as they recede after tooth extraction, causing the denture to fit differently. Regular dental checkups allow Dr. Brown to monitor the fit and recommend relining or replacement when necessary to keep your jaw alignment correct and your denture comfortable.

What are implant-supported dentures?

Implant-supported dentures attach to dental implants placed in the jawbone, providing a dramatically more secure and stable fit than traditional dentures. They eliminate slipping, improve chewing function, preserve jawbone, and feel much more like natural teeth. Dr. Brown will assess your suitability for implant-supported dentures — including jawbone density and overall health — during your consultation.

Do I still need dental checkups if I have dentures?

Yes — absolutely. Regular dental examinations remain important for denture wearers, even when no natural teeth remain. At each visit, Dr. Brown checks your oral tissues for signs of disease or change, evaluates the fit and condition of your denture, and assesses whether relining or replacement is needed. Catching problems early prevents discomfort and more costly treatment later.

Are dentures covered by dental insurance?

Many dental insurance plans provide partial coverage for dentures. Coverage amounts, annual maximums, and waiting periods vary by plan. Our administrative team will verify your specific benefits before treatment begins and provide you with a transparent cost estimate. We accept most major dental insurance plans and offer flexible financing for any remaining balance.

How do I know if dentures or dental implants are right for me?

Both dentures and dental implants are excellent solutions for missing teeth. The best option depends on the number of missing teeth, the condition of your remaining jawbone, your overall health and suitability for surgery, your budget and insurance coverage, and your preference for a removable vs. fixed solution. Many patients opt for implant-supported dentures — which combine the coverage of a denture with the stability of implants. During your consultation, Dr. Brown will review all options with you and help you make the best decision for your lifestyle and long-term dental health.
